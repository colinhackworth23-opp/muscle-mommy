How to check for doming
This is the check the whole phase progression depends on.

Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat. Place your fingertips just above your belly button, along the midline. Lift your head and shoulders slightly off the floor, like the start of a crunch.

What you're feeling for: a ridge or cone pushing up along the center line, or a gap between the muscle edges that your fingers sink into. A soft gap of 1–2 fingers is common and usually fine. A visible ridge that pops up under load is the thing to watch.

During exercise: glance down at your midline during core work. If you see a cone or ridge push up, that movement is too much right now — regress it or reduce range. Doming isn't damage, it's feedback.

Finding your pelvic floor

The contraction is a lift and squeeze inward and upward — the feeling of stopping gas, then drawing up. Not a clench of the glutes, not a breath hold, not a suck-in of the belly.

The release matters as much as the lift. Let go fully between every rep and feel the drop back to neutral. A floor that can't relax is as much of a problem as one that can't contract.

Breathe throughout. Exhale as you lift, inhale as you release.

Important: This protocol assumes a weak pelvic floor. Some postpartum floors are the opposite — too tight — and produce the same symptoms of leaking and pain. For those, strengthening work makes things worse. Nobody can tell which from the outside. If symptoms aren't improving after a few weeks, or if anything hurts, that's the signal to get assessed rather than push harder.
Worth doing: a pelvic floor PT visit

A single evaluation gives you an actual diastasis measurement, tells you whether your floor is weak or tight, and turns this program from general principles into something built for your body. It's routine postpartum care in France and Australia and badly underused here. Ask your OB for a referral.

Joint laxity

Relaxin keeps joints looser than usual for months after birth, and longer while breastfeeding. Stretch to moderate range, not end range — especially hips, low back, and pelvis. If a stretch feels great in the moment, it's probably deeper than it should be right now.

Stop and get checked if
  • Bleeding restarts or increases after activity
  • Heaviness, bulging, or dragging in the pelvis or vagina
  • Pain — pelvic, abdominal, low back, or pubic bone
  • Leaking that isn't improving over several weeks
  • A visible ridge along the midline that doesn't go away with regression
